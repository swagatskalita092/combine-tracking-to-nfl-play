Reviewer scope: writeup/writeup.md, notebook, scripts 08-14, outputs/*.csv, nb_run logs, notes 05 and 07. Project files were not modified. ## Verdict

The arithmetic is clean: every number in Tables 1-2, the sensor-arm CIs and the descriptive counts reproduce from outputs/*.csv and the nb_run logs, the leave-one-player-out (LOO) design is genuinely out-of-sample for the test player, early and late plays never overlap, and my permutation check shows the procedure is not anti-conservative. The weaknesses are in what the numbers are said to mean and in how the claims are labelled. (1) The headline credits "Combine speed", but the prior contains body weight, and a weight-only prior does as well as or better than the full prior for DL top speed, WR top speed and WR separation. (2) "Pre-registered" is stronger than the record supports for test 2 (file timestamps put the results before the stated registration time; the registration is a self-written text file), and the author's own robustness note (notes/12) says WR top speed fails Holm correction and DL get-off is not met in 0/5 seeds, yet the writeup still shows "Met"/"borderline", says no multiplicity correction was applied, and does not mention it. (3) The headline raw-versus-Combine error comparison (line 12) is mostly a shrinkage effect, is dominated by noise in the 100-play target, and for WR separation the Combine increment over a position-mean prior is not statistically distinguishable from zero. A judge who reads the code will find these quickly. The honest-null framing is a real strength; the entry should lean on it and tighten the claims.

## Critical issues

**C1. "Combine speed" is not isolated; weight carries most or all of the gain.** The Combine prior is (forty, ten_yd_split, combine_weight) (scripts/12 line 5). Post-hoc ablation using the same LOO code (MSE vs last-100 mean; position-mean prior in brackets):

| Outcome | k | position mean | full prior | weight only | forty + 10yd only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DL top speed | 0 | .2835 | .1398 | .1356 | .1675 |
| DL top speed | 25 | .1843 | .1353 | .1330 | .1526 |
| WR top speed | 0 | .1193 | .0778 | .0872 | .1020 |
| WR top speed | 25 | .1006 | .0758 | .0800 | .0931 |
| WR separation | 0 | .0495 | .0381 | .0342 | .0544 |
| WR separation | 25 | .0448 | .0392 | .0358 | .0488 |
| DL get-off | 0 | .0110 | .0073 | .0079 | .0071 |

For DL top speed weight alone beats the full prior; for WR separation weight alone beats it and speed-only is worse than the position mean. Weight likely proxies edge-vs-interior (DL) and slot-vs-outside/size (WR), i.e. role, not athleticism (notes/06 line 21 already suspected this for DL get-off; the writeup drops it). Affected: line 9 ("Combine speed is worth ... 27 snaps"), title ("What is a Combine sensor reading worth"), line 94 and Figure 4 (the "sensor" prior is tracked vmax + v10 + weight; weight is not a sensor reading). Safer wording: "A Combine-informed prior (40 time, 10-yd split, weight) is worth about 27 snaps for DL top speed, versus 9 for the position mean; most of that gain is attributable to weight, which probably reflects role (edge vs interior) rather than speed." Add this ablation (it is cheap) or at minimum state the limitation.

**C2. "Pre-registered" label is not supported at the strength claimed, and the notebook contradicts the writeup.**
- notes/07 header says "written before running, Nov 26 2026 ~23:15 IST" (= 17:45 UTC). File mtimes: scripts/12_snaps_equivalent.py 17:37:42 UTC, outputs/snaps_equivalent_results.csv 17:37:54 UTC, notes/08_snaps_equivalent_results.md 17:38:24 UTC (= 23:07-23:08 IST). The results precede the stated registration time by about 7 minutes. notes/07 itself was edited later (addendum; mtime 18:28 UTC), so its mtime proves nothing either way, but the record as it stands cannot demonstrate that the design preceded the run. Mtimes can be wrong (mounted D: drive), so treat this as "unprovable", not "proven false"; fix by being precise, not by asserting.
- Both registrations are self-authored text files with self-reported times, not an external timestamped registry. Subtitle (line 3), headings (lines 35, 54) and line 6 imply more than that.
- Line 37 "Before opening any NFL outcome": notes/02 (16:26) and notes/03 (16:41) summarised outcome distributions (OL pressure rate 8.5%, SD .027 vs .013, coverage, routes per player) before notes/05 (16:59). No outcome-vs-Combine association was looked at, so the defensible wording is "before relating any NFL outcome to Combine features".
- Line 86 correctly says registration 2 was written after test 1's null. But notebook cell 0 (scripts/15_build_notebook.py line 16) says "Design (pre-registered before any outcome was looked at, see Writeup)", which contradicts line 86. Fix the notebook text.
Safer wording: "Design and decision rules were written down in dated notes before the analysis they govern (test 1) / before the final run (test 2; the file timestamps cannot exclude that the first run came first); they are self-recorded, not registered with a third party."

**C3. Claims shown as established that the author's own robustness analysis does not support.** notes/12 + outputs/robustness_holm.csv: pre-set rule met in 5/5 reseeded bootstraps for DL and WR top speed, 0/5 for DL get-off; intersection-union p for WR top speed = .0128 vs Holm threshold .0083 (not rejected); only DL top speed survives Holm across the 7 pre-registered outcomes. notes/12 line 14 says "Writeup must say so". The writeup does not: line 9 lists WR top speed 25 snaps and DL get-off 16 snaps as findings; Table 2 shows "Met" for WR top speed and "Borderline" for DL get-off; Figure 3 labels "pre-set test passed"/"borderline"; line 99 says "we apply no multiplicity correction". Also my read of the primary CSV: WR top speed k=0 CI lower bound is .0041 (outputs/snaps_equivalent_results.csv) but .0033 for the identical quantity in sensor_vs_stopwatch.csv (gain_stop_vs_pos), i.e. the lower bound moves with the RNG stream; it is near zero either way. Safer wording for Table 2 and line 9: "DL top speed: rule met and survives Holm correction across the seven registered outcomes. WR top speed: rule met, but does not survive Holm (p = .013 vs .008); treat as suggestive. DL get-off: rule not met (k = 25 lower bound -0.00004; 0 of 5 reseeded runs)." Change "borderline" to "not met" (the pre-registered rule is binary, notes/07 line 34). Note Holm was added post hoc; say so.

**C4. Headline error comparison overstates what the Combine contributes (lines 12 and 62, Figure 2).** Line 12: raw .356 vs Combine-informed .040 (WR separation, k = 10). The same CSV gives position-mean shrinkage .0503 at k = 10, so nearly all of the improvement is shrinkage, not Combine; the Combine increment is .0106 with CI [-.0020, .0249] (outputs/snaps_equivalent_results.csv). Same pattern for DL top speed k = 10 (raw .510, position .248, Combine .152): here the increment is real but only about 27% of the total drop (.096 of .358). Second, these MSEs include the sampling noise of the target (mean of 100 plays): sigma^2/100 = .0324 for WR separation, so ~81% of ".040" is irreducible; for DL top speed .0196 of .152 is noise floor (13%), at k = 100 WR separation .0382 vs floor .0324. "Misses by .040" is therefore not an error of the estimate in any practical sense. Third, line 12 and Figure 2's axis ("next-100-play average") say "next 100"; the code uses the LAST 100 plays of the whole observed career (scripts/12 line 58), which for a player with 1,000 snaps is many games later. Safer wording for line 12: "Averaging a receiver's first 10 routes misses his last-100-route average by .356 yd^2; shrinking toward the position average cuts that to .050, and a Combine-informed prior to .040 (difference between the last two not distinguishable from zero; about .032 of any such error is noise in the 100-route target itself)."

## Moderate issues

**M1. Test 1 rule is misstated, a null is over-read, and some pre-registered CIs are omitted.**
- Line 37 says the rule was "a bootstrap interval for the cross-validated gain excludes zero". notes/05 line 19 says "bootstrap CI for the gain/partial correlation". scripts/10 computes a permutation p for the joint gain and bootstrap CIs only for partial correlations (lines 46-55); there is no bootstrap CI for CV gain anywhere.
- Applying notes/05's rule literally, partial-correlation CIs that exclude 0 for primary outcomes exist (log s07: DL top speed peak_acc +.35 [+.03,+.61]; pooled d95 [+.00,+.27]; WR separation peak_acc -.20 [-.42,-.00]). notes/06 line 16 discusses these; the writeup (line 52) mentions only the p = .038. The conclusion (chance) is reasonable, but the registered rule would have licensed a claim; say so and explain why you did not follow it.
- Line 11 "added nothing" and Table 1: CIs are wide (partial-corr half-width about .25 at n about 60; notes/06 line 17 "inconclusive, not proof of absence" did not make it into the writeup). Feature reliabilities of .46-.50 are single-run test-retest; the features entering the model are two-run means, whose Spearman-Brown reliability is about .63-.67 (not "unreliable", line 31). Safer: "no detectable gain; with about 60 players per outcome we could not rule out modest gains."
- Deviations from notes/05 not disclosed: O1 is unweighted (scripts/10 lines 63, 66 use np.ones) while notes/05 line 18 says exposure-weighted; minimum-exposure thresholds (O1 >= 100 plays, O2/O3 >= 50, O4 >= 100) are not in the registration.

**M2. Sensor/stopwatch agreement is overstated (lines 11, 92-94, Figure 4).**
- The -0.98 (outputs/13.log; scripts/13 line 15) pools all positions. Within position, same variables (my recomputation, tracked mean-run vmax vs official forty): DB -.81 (n=102), WR -.83 (94), OL -.91 (95), TE -.91 (34), DL -.98 (93). Tracked 10-yd speed vs official 10-yd split: pooled -.90 but within position DB -.42, WR -.17, TE -.40, OL -.64, DL -.88. The sensor prior's v10 term is therefore essentially unrelated to the stopwatch split for receivers.
- Line 92 "The two priors explain the same share of player-to-player variation in every outcome" is false for WR top speed (34% vs 40%, visible in Figure 4) and DB (0% vs 2%); n0 25 vs 28.
- Line 94 "the tracking system is a valid way to get the speed signal" and "(418 vs 424; no extra players)": agreement is shown only for the 40-dash vmax, mostly between positions. "Valid" is a measurement claim not tested here (no external criterion). Safer: "Tracked top speed in the 40 correlates -0.98 with the official time across all positions (-0.81 to -0.98 within position)".
- DL get-off: the sensor prior is worse than the stopwatch prior at every k (MSE k = 0 .00803 vs .00733, 10% worse; CI [-.0016, +.0001], upper bound .0001). "Indistinguishable" is right, but the direction is consistent and worth a sentence.
- Figure 4 shows no uncertainty; it plots point estimates of shares (see M3 for their CIs).

**M3. "Snaps the prior is worth" is model-based, is mis-attributed in the bullet, and has no uncertainty.**
- n0 = sigma^2/tau^2 is the weight (in play-level iid units) that the whole prior carries against the player's own mean; n0_comb = sigma^2/tau_r^2 uses the residual variance after the Combine regression. It is correct as a shrinkage-weight definition (scripts/12 lines 46, 67) and the posterior formula at line 63 is the right normal-normal posterior. But the writeup's phrase "how many NFL snaps of evidence the Combine reading counts for" (line 7) and "27 snaps" attribute the whole prior weight, including what a position average already supplies (9), to the Combine; the Combine increment is 18 snaps, and, per C1, it is largely weight.
- Assumes iid plays within a player (sigma^2 pooled play-level variance, no game or opponent clustering, no development trend over a rookie year). n0 is in different units across outcomes (top speed: any play with >= 20 tracking frames, including non-pass plays; get-off and separation: pass snaps only); "one game's worth of snaps" (line 80) is therefore approximate.
- No CIs on n0 or "share explained". Player-bootstrap over the pool (my check, B = 300, 95% percentile): DL top speed share .56-.79, n0_comb 20-44; WR top speed share .22-.54, n0_comb 18-46; DL get-off share .34-.68, n0_comb 12-25; WR separation share .29-.73, n0_comb 106-539 (point 174). Report these or at least say the point estimates are imprecise by a factor of about 2.
- Table 2 shares and n0 are in-sample pool estimates (test players are a subset of the pool), not LOO quantities; say so.

**M4. Selection and timing.** Test players are defined by having >= 200 qualifying plays over the whole window (scripts/12 line 43), i.e. conditioned on future playing time; pool is >= 30 plays; test n is 37-55 and mostly earlier draft classes. Statements about "a rookie in September" (line 16) extrapolate to players who stayed on the field. LOO removes the test player from the prior, but other players' contemporaneous (including later) plays, from the same games and opponents, are used: it is out-of-sample across players, not out-of-time across the league; "out of time" (line 58) holds only within player. WR separation adjustment (global OLS on route, coverage, alignment, cushion; scripts/12 lines 14-16) is fit once on all plays including each test player's late plays (small, but it is leakage of the target into the target construction; also no defender, QB, depth-of-target or game context). Line 100 acknowledges survivorship partially; add "test players are those with >= 200 snaps".

**M5. Football validity and recommendation language.**
- Per-play max speed is not role-adjusted and is not a performance outcome; DB top speed and OL top speed (Table 2) are not meaningful football outcomes (DBs run to react; OL rarely sprint), so "For OL and DB, we found no usable Combine signal in any outcome we tested" (line 10) is much narrower than it reads: DB has one outcome (top speed), OL has top speed and pressure allowed. No coverage/tackle/EPA outcome for DB. Safer: "For OL and DB the outcomes we could test (top speed; OL pressure allowed) showed no signal."
- Position comes from the Combine label (combine_position), not play-level alignment; DL = edge + interior, WR = X/slot, so prior and outcome are confounded by role (see C1).
- Lines 78-82 ("Reading the results as a coach or analyst would", "A prior worth 27 snaps is a different tool from one worth 3", "which is what a good prior should do") read as usage guidance. Nothing here tests a scouting decision, a roster outcome, or calibrated decision value. Safer: "If the model holds, the Combine-informed prior would carry about as much weight as 27 plays of a DL's own top-speed data."
- Line 81 "largest in football terms" for WR separation (174 routes) while the CI includes 0 at every k and n0 CI is 106-539: this is the weakest-evidence result presented as the biggest effect; consider dropping "largest in football terms".

**M6. Reproducibility statement vs what the notebook does.** Line 106 says the notebook "reproduces every figure and table here". Not in the notebook: script 02 (OL coverage 93%, SD .027 vs .013; line 82 and line 102), script 16 (Holm), the "0.3 s slower" claim and the "about 8%" `dis` claim (no code computes either). My recomputation: `dis` under-counts by 10.5% in 40-yard-dash events and about 39% across all drills; tracked time to 38 yd from motion onset exceeds the official 40 by 0.13 s on average (sd .16), not 0.3 s (0.3 s may hold only after extrapolating 2 yd; state it). scripts/03 loops over DL, WR, DB, TE only, so OL pass-protection counts (66 players >= 100 snaps) are not produced by the notebook either. The rerun in nb_run is otherwise hash-identical to outputs/ for all result CSVs (good).

**M7. OL pressure spread mislabelled (line 82).** ".027" (notes/02 line 6) is the observed SD of player rates, which includes sampling noise; the estimated true between-player SD is .023 (log s07, players >= 100) or .0175 (outputs/snaps_equivalent_results.csv tau_tot, pool >= 30). So the true spread is about 1.3-1.8 times the sampling noise (.013), not "about twice".

**M8. Variance-component estimator has an unregistered constant.** fit_prior (scripts/12 lines 27-30) weights with 1/(0.5*tt + sigma^2/n) and applies N/(N-p) to squared residuals; notes/07 says only "method-of-moments". The 0.5 is an ad hoc constant. It is applied identically to every arm, so it cannot favour the Combine prior, but it is an undocumented analyst choice. See "Things verified OK" for the 2% floor.
